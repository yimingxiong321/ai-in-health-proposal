\section*{Problem and why it matters}
Clinical LLM agents can diagnose patients and, between encounters, revise a persistent
\emph{skill library} instead of their weights. In prior work (\textsc{DxSkill}, submitted
to ACL ARR), a student revising its own skills on MIMIC-CDM improves from 77.5\% to
86.9--89.1\% depending on feedback, and a stronger reviser helps mainly when feedback is
weak. Skill revision is also not reliably safe: a useful skill can be silently deleted,
and a transient tool error can harden into a permanent restriction, even with outcome
feedback available. These findings motivate two separate, \emph{independent} follow-up
tracks (A and B), not one combined study.

\textbf{Direction A} (inspired by OpenAI's 2023 weak-to-strong generalization work): can a
weaker model teach a stronger diagnostic agent by authoring and maintaining the skill
library the strong model executes, rather than supervising its predictions directly?
Crossing diagnoser and reviser strength separates task-execution ability from
reflection/knowledge-organization ability. \textbf{Direction B}: can skill revision be
made safer by validating each proposed update on a holdout buffer before committing it,
so useful skills aren't deleted and transient errors aren't hardened into restrictions?

\section*{Data, and whether we have access}
MIMIC-CDM 1.1 (from MIMIC-IV): 250 training / 400 held-out patients across appendicitis,
cholecystitis, diverticulitis, and pancreatitis. We already have data access (completed
MIMIC data-use training) and a working DxSkill pipeline (diagnosis tools,
reflection/revision loop, checkpointed libraries), so no new access or infrastructure is
needed to begin either direction.

\section*{Approach and baselines}
\textbf{A -- weak-to-strong skill transfer.} Baseline: DxSkill's student-self-revises
(86.9\%) and teacher-edits-weak-student (Qwen3.8-27B revises, Qwen3-30B-A3B diagnoses)
conditions. New: a full diagnoser$\times$reviser strength grid, \{weak,strong\} $\times$
\{weak,strong\}, with feedback fixed (reference diagnosis). The weak-revises-strong cell
is the key untested condition.

\textbf{B -- validation-buffer gating.} Baseline: DxSkill's current ungated revision. New:
evaluate each proposed update on a small fixed holdout buffer (disjoint from the eval set)
before committing it; accept only if buffer accuracy doesn't regress beyond tolerance
$\varepsilon$, else roll back to the prior checkpoint.

\section*{How we'll evaluate it}
\textbf{A:} accuracy on the frozen 400-patient set per diagnoser$\times$reviser cell
(paired patient-bootstrap intervals, as in our ACL ARR submission), to locate where
weak-to-strong transfer helps or fails.
\textbf{B:} buffer-set accuracy per checkpoint to track accept/reject decisions; held-out
accuracy with vs.\ without the buffer; qualitative tracing of accepted/rejected edits,
counting how often the buffer blocks a harmful edit or preserves a useful one (cf.\ our
Figure 7 case).
