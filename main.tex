\section*{Problem and why it matters}
Clinical LLM agents diagnose patients and, between encounters, revise a persistent
\emph{skill library} instead of their weights. \textbf{Prior work:} in \textsc{DxSkill}
(currently under review at ACL ARR), we found accuracy depends heavily on feedback
(77.5\%$\to$86.9--89.1\%) and that skill revision is not reliably safe -- a useful skill
can be silently deleted, and a transient tool error can harden into a permanent
restriction, even with outcome feedback available. \textbf{New this semester:} we pursue
two independent directions, (A) weak-to-strong skill transfer and (B) buffer-validated
skill updates, described below. Krishanu and Lakshmi are also extending DxSkill; we are
coordinating with them on which experiments each team runs to avoid duplicating work.

\textbf{Direction A} (inspired by OpenAI's 2023 weak-to-strong generalization work): can a
weaker model teach a stronger diagnostic agent by authoring and maintaining the skill
library the strong model executes, rather than supervising its predictions directly?
\textbf{Direction B}: can skill revision be made safer by validating each proposed update
on a holdout buffer before committing it?

\section*{Data, access, and code}
MIMIC-CDM 1.1 / MIMIC-IV-Ext-CDM: 250 training / 400 held-out patients across
appendicitis, cholecystitis, diverticulitis, and pancreatitis. Each team member is
obtaining individual PhysioNet credentialing for MIMIC-IV-Ext-CDM (beyond our shared ACL
ARR access). Code requirement: a Colab notebook that recomputes all reported metrics from
our saved experiment logs; no raw case text appears in the logs we share, slides, or
report.

\section*{Approach and baselines}
We define ``weak'' and ``strong'' by each model's measured no-skill accuracy on MIMIC-CDM
(DxSkill Table 1), not by parameter count: \textbf{weak} = Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507-FP8
(77.5\%); \textbf{strong} = Qwen3.8-27B-FP8 with thinking enabled (85.1\%).

\textbf{A -- weak-to-strong skill transfer (primary direction).} Baseline: DxSkill's
student-self-revises (86.9\%) and teacher-edits-weak-student conditions. New: the full
diagnoser$\times$reviser grid, \{weak,strong\}$\times$\{weak,strong\}, feedback fixed
(reference diagnosis), \textbf{2 seeds per cell} since skill revision is sensitive to
update order.

\textbf{B -- validation-buffer gating (secondary direction).} Baseline: DxSkill's current
ungated revision. New: a fixed holdout buffer of patients drawn from the training pool,
disjoint from the training stream and the 400-patient eval set. We compare \textbf{two
buffer sizes} (40 and 100 patients) crossed with an $\varepsilon$ tolerance sweep, since a
small buffer makes accept/reject decisions noisy; accept an update only if buffer accuracy
doesn't regress beyond $\varepsilon$, else roll back to the prior checkpoint.

\section*{How we'll evaluate it}
\textbf{A:} accuracy on the frozen 400-patient set per diagnoser$\times$reviser cell,
averaged over seeds, with paired patient-bootstrap intervals (as in our ACL ARR
submission), to locate where weak-to-strong transfer helps or fails.
\textbf{B:} buffer-set accuracy per checkpoint across both buffer sizes; held-out accuracy
with vs.\ without the buffer; qualitative tracing of accepted/rejected edits (cf.\ our
Figure 7 case).
